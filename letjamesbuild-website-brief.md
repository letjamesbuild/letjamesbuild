# LetJamesBuild.com — Website Brief

## Overview

Personal brand website for James Radford (@letjamesbuild). This is a creator portfolio and content hub, NOT a consultancy or agency website. The tone is "a builder sharing what he knows" — think personal blog meets portfolio meets subtle lead generation.

The site must feel like a person, not a company. No service tiers. No pricing pages. No "our team" section. No consultancy language. Just James, what he builds, what he's learned, and a way to get in touch.

---

## Strategic Intent (Internal — Do Not Display)

- Primary traffic: LinkedIn profile clicks, TikTok/Shorts bio links, X profile links
- Primary conversion: Book a call via Calendly embed or link
- Secondary conversion: Follow on socials, subscribe to newsletter/content
- Positioning: AI builder and creator who shares knowledge — NOT an AI consultancy
- Must pass the "boss test": if an employer sees this, it looks like a personal portfolio, not a competing business

---

## Design Direction

### Aesthetic
- Dark, modern, minimal. Think personal dev portfolio meets creator site.
- Not corporate. Not agency. Not startup landing page.
- Inspired by: personal sites of indie hackers, solo founders, technical creators
- Monospace accents for technical credibility, clean sans-serif for body
- Subtle animations on scroll. Nothing flashy. Confident and understated.

### Color Palette
- Background: near-black (#0F0E0C or similar)
- Text: warm off-white (#E8E4DD)
- Accent: muted gold (#C4A265) — used sparingly for CTAs and highlights
- Secondary: slate/charcoal for cards and sections (#1A1815, #2A2722)

### Typography
- Headings: A distinctive display font (e.g., Playfair Display or similar serif for contrast)
- Body: Clean sans-serif (DM Sans, Satoshi, or similar)
- Code/labels: Monospace (DM Mono, JetBrains Mono)

### Layout
- Single page with smooth scroll sections, OR minimal multi-page (home + 1-2 subpages max)
- Mobile-first. Most traffic will come from social media on phones.
- Fast. No heavy frameworks if possible. Static site preferred (Next.js static export, Astro, or plain HTML/CSS/JS).

## Site Structure

### 1. Hero Section

**Headline:**
"I build things with AI. Then I share how."

**Subtext:**
"I'm James. I ship AI products, run agents, and document what actually works. No hype, no fluff — just what I'm building and learning."

**Visual:** Clean photo of James (use the quarterly event photos when available, or a professional headshot). Not a stock image. Not an illustration. A real person.

**CTAs:**
- Primary: "See what I'm building" (scrolls to projects)
- Secondary: Social links — LinkedIn, X (@letjamesbuild), TikTok

**Note:** No mention of consulting, services, training, or anything commercial. This is a creator introduction.

### 3. How I Think (Content Section)

**Section Header:** "How I Think"

This is the content hub. It serves two purposes:
1. SEO and credibility for people arriving from search
2. A place to link to from social posts ("full breakdown on my site")

Format: Blog-style cards or a clean list of articles/essays. Each has a title, a 1-2 line description, and a read link.

**Initial content to seed (can be placeholder until written):**

- "How I Build Production Software with Zero Developers"
  "The spec-driven, multi-agent process behind CallMilly.com."

- "What Actually Works with AI in 2026"
  "After building multiple AI products, here's what I've learned about what delivers value and what's just noise."

- "Distribution-First Thinking for AI Products"
  "Why most AI products fail on distribution, not technology."

- "The Founder's Guide to Using AI Tools"
  "A practical, no-jargon overview of what's worth your time right now."

**Note:** The "Founder's Guide to Using AI Tools" piece is the bridge content — it's genuinely useful, it positions James as someone who helps founders with AI, and it naturally creates inbound interest without being a sales page. Someone reads this and thinks "this guy knows his stuff, I should talk to him" — which is the whole point.

**Section Header:** "About" or just "James"

Keep this short and human. Not a CV dump. A few paragraphs that tell the story.

**Draft copy:**

"I've been building internet businesses since I was 20. I started with a music talent platform that grew to a £1.6m raise, then spent years doing growth and marketing for tech companies and global brands, and now I build AI products.

I use AI agents to ship full-stack software without a dev team. Not as a novelty — as my actual development process. Milly, my AI recruitment agent, was built entirely this way.

I live in Norwich, I don't drink, I have a dog, and I spend most of my time either building or figuring out what to build next. If you want to talk about AI, building things, or anything else, the best way to reach me is below."

**What this does NOT include:**
- No mention of the venture studio or employment
- No mention of consulting or services
- No mention of crypto (unless in passing as "tech companies")
- No "hire me" language

---

### 5. Social Proof / Credibility Strip

A subtle horizontal strip or grid showing credibility markers. Not a "logos" section — more like quick facts.

**Examples:**
- "£1.6m raised for a consumer platform at £15m valuation"
- "Led campaigns for AAVE, Evian, McLaren"
- "10,000+ users onboarded in 48 hours"
- "PRINCE2 & SCRUM certified"
- "Comic Relief Social Enterprise of the Year"
- "Everest Base Camp. Sahara Desert. 100-mile march."

These should feel like texture, not bragging. Small font, understated design, horizontal scroll on mobile.

---

### 6. Call to Action Section

**Section Header:** "Let's Talk"

**Copy:**
"If you're building something interesting, want to jam on AI, or just want to say hi — I'm always up for a conversation."

**CTA Button:** "Book a Call" → Links to Calendly

**Secondary:** "Or DM me on LinkedIn / X"

**What this does NOT say:**
- No "hire me for consulting"
- No "book a free consultation"
- No "get your AI audit"
- No pricing, packages, or service descriptions

It's just a person inviting a conversation. The commercial conversion happens IN the call, not on the website. The site's job is to get them to book. The call's job is to sell.

Minimal footer with:

## Pages

### Option A: Single Page (Recommended for V1)
Everything above as sections on one scrolling page. Clean, fast, mobile-optimised. Most social media traffic expects a single page they can scroll through in 30 seconds.

### Option B: Multi-page (V2 if content grows)
- Home (hero + projects + credibility + CTA)
- /writing (content hub with articles)
- /about (extended bio)

Start with Option A. Add pages later as content library grows.

## Technical Requirements

- Static site. No server-side rendering needed for V1.
- Recommended stack: Next.js static export, Astro, or plain HTML/CSS/JS
- Hosted on Vercel or Netlify (free tier is fine)
- Custom domain: letjamesbuild.com
- SSL enabled (automatic with Vercel/Netlify)
- Mobile-first responsive design
- Fast load times (<2s on mobile)
- Open Graph meta tags for social sharing (so LinkedIn/X previews look good)
- Calendly embed or link for CTA
- Google Analytics or Plausible for basic tracking
- No cookies banner needed if using Plausible (cookie-free analytics)

---

## The Peter Test

If Peter Damm visits this site, he sees:
- James has a personal portfolio showing his AI projects
- James writes about AI and building things
- James has a "let's talk" button like every other person in tech
- There is zero mention of consulting, training, services, pricing, or anything that could be interpreted as running a competing business

His reaction: "Oh, James made a personal site. Whatever." Not: "James is running a business behind my back."

That's the line. Stay on the right side of it until the timing changes.
